WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
Japanese candlestick patterns are one of the oldest and most powerful tools in
technical analysis. Each candle tells a story about who's in control of the market
— buyers or sellers. When specific patterns form at key levels, they signal a
high-probability shift in market direction.
This guide covers 8 reversal patterns grouped into single-candle and
multi-candle formations. Master these and you'll start seeing the market the way
professional traders do.

 Single-Candle Patterns
DOJI
 ■ REVERSAL
 SIGNAL
The Doji is one of the most important Japanese
candlestick patterns. It forms when the market opens
and closes at the same price — meaning total equality
and indecision between buyers and sellers.
When a Doji appears after a strong uptrend or
downtrend, it signals that the previous momentum is
exhausted. The market is pausing and a reversal is
likely incoming.
KEY POINTS
 Open price ≈ Close price
 Long wicks on both sides = battle between buyers and sellers
 Strongest signal when it appears at support or resistance
GRAVESTONE DOJI
 ■ BEARISH
REVERSAL
The Gravestone Doji is the bearish version of the
Dragonfly Doji. It forms when the open, low, and close
are all at roughly the same price — but the market
pushed significantly higher before getting rejected.
The long upper tail shows that buyers tried to push
price up but sellers slammed it back down. Strong
signal of trend exhaustion at the top of an uptrend.
KEY POINTS
 Long upper wick — buyers rejected at the top
 Tiny or no lower wick
 Best signal: at resistance after an uptrend

DRAGONFLY DOJI
 ■ BULLISH
REVERSAL
The Dragonfly Doji is a bullish reversal pattern that
forms when the open, high, and close are at roughly
the same price, with a long lower wick.
The long lower tail shows that sellers tried to push price
down but buyers stepped in aggressively and
pushed it back up. Classic sign that buyers are taking
control.
KEY POINTS
 Long lower wick — sellers rejected at the bottom
 Tiny or no upper wick
 Best signal: at support after a downtrend
HAMMER
 ■ BULLISH
REVERSAL
The Hammer is created when the open, high, and close
are roughly the same price, with a long lower shadow.
It signals strong bullish rejection from buyers.
Forms at the bottom of a downtrend. Sellers pushed
the market lower after the open, but buyers stepped in
hard and pushed price back up to close near the high.
The market is signaling: the bottom is in.
KEY POINTS
 Small body at the top, long lower wick
 Wick should be at least 2x the body size
 Strongest at support levels after a downtrend

SHOOTING STAR
 ■ BEARISH
REVERSAL
The Shooting Star is the bearish version of the
Hammer. It forms when open, low, and close are
roughly the same price, with a long upper shadow.
Buyers tried to push the market higher, but sellers
rejected them aggressively and slammed price back
down. Professional technicians say the shadow should
be at least twice the length of the real body. Forms at
the top of an uptrend.
KEY POINTS
 Small body at the bottom, long upper wick
 Wick should be at least 2x the body size
 Strongest at resistance levels after an uptrend
 Multi-Candle Patterns
MORNING STAR
 ■ BULLISH
REVERSAL
The Morning Star is a powerful 3-candle bullish
reversal pattern that forms at the bottom of a
downtrend.
1st candle: Big bearish — sellers still in control.
2nd candle: Small body (often a Doji) — sellers losing
momentum.
3rd candle: Big bullish — closes above the midpoint of
candle 1. Buyers have taken over.
KEY POINTS
 Best when 3rd candle closes deep into 1st candle's body
 Works strongest near support levels
 Signals a powerful trend reversal up

EVENING STAR
 ■ BEARISH
REVERSAL
The Evening Star is the mirror image of the Morning
Star — a powerful 3-candle bearish reversal that
forms at the top of an uptrend.
1st candle: Big bullish — buyers still in control.
2nd candle: Small body — buyers losing steam.
3rd candle: Big bearish — closes below the midpoint
of candle 1. Sellers have taken over.
KEY POINTS
 Best when 3rd candle closes deep into 1st candle's body
 Works strongest near resistance levels
 Signals a powerful trend reversal down
TWEEZER TOPS
 ■ BEARISH
REVERSAL
Tweezer Tops form at the top of an uptrend. Two
candlesticks side by side with matching highs — a
bullish candle followed by a bearish one.
Buyers pushed price higher giving the impression the
uptrend continues — but sellers ambushed them at
the same exact high level and pushed price back down.
Clear sign that buyers can't break that resistance.
KEY POINTS
 Two candles with matching highs (the 'tweezers')
 1st bullish, 2nd bearish
 Strongest at resistance after an uptrend

TWEEZER BOTTOMS
 ■ BULLISH
REVERSAL
Tweezer Bottoms form at the bottom of a downtrend.
Two candlesticks side by side with matching lows — a
bearish candle followed by a bullish one.
Sellers pushed price lower giving the impression the
downtrend continues — but buyers ambushed them
at the same exact low level and pushed price back up
above the bearish candle's close. Clear sign that
sellers can't break that support.
KEY POINTS
 Two candles with matching lows
 1st bearish, 2nd bullish
 Strongest at support after a downtrend

GOLDEN RULES FOR USING CANDLESTICK PATTERNS
1. Patterns are signals, not guarantees — always confirm with other tools.
2. Strongest at support and resistance levels — context matters more than the
pattern itself.
3. Higher timeframe patterns are more reliable than lower timeframes.
4. Wait for the candle to close before acting — don't predict.
5. Combine with volume and trend direction for the highest probability setups.
6. A pattern in the middle of nowhere = noise. A pattern at a key level = signal.
 Read the candles. Read the market. Hunt with the whales.
