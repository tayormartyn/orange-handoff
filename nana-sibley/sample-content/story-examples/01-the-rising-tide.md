# Worked Example 01 — "The Rising Tide"

**The gold-standard reference for how a family piece must feel.**
Format: `multi_character` · Nan → Maeve → Jesse → Nan · a gentle dream.

> Dream input: *"I was swimming in a rising, deep-blue ocean under a massive,
> silent full moon. The water felt warm, but no matter which way I turned, I
> couldn't see the shore. I wasn't drowning — I was just floating, looking
> for land, waiting for something to appear."*

---

### NAN SYBIL — *fireside; warm open, then she drops into the stillness*

> "Oh… there you are. Come in, love — sit down by the fire. You've brought me a good one tonight."
>
> *(slow breath)*
>
> "You were out in the deep water. The blue… and that big, silent moon. And no shore, whichever way you turned."
>
> *(gravity point — she goes quiet before the weight)*
>
> "But here's the first thing I want you to hear, my darling.
> You weren't drowning.
> …You were floating.
>
> That matters more than you know."
>
> *warm, a small nod* — "Now, the water — that belongs to our Maeve. She feels these things before the rest of us even notice them. Maeve, love?"

### MAEVE — *close, moonlit; intimate stillness, held pauses*

> "Mm. I felt that one the moment she read it.
>
> Some of the old stories say water is feeling, sweet thing — all of it. And a warm, rising, deep-blue sea… that isn't fear. That's a lot of feeling you've been carrying, gently, for a long while now.
>
> And the moon — silent, full, watching over it? Some say that's the thing you *feel*… but haven't let yourself say out loud. Even to you.
>
> So notice, softly — you weren't thrashing. You were floating. Looking for land. *Waiting for something to appear.*
>
> …Can I ask you something tender? What have you been waiting for someone — or something — to come along and show you… when a part of you already knows?"
>
> *she turns, gently handing it on* — "That's the feeling of it. But our Jesse… he's the one who helps you actually swim. Jess."

### JESSE — *grounded, leans in; one true sentence lands, then the one ask*

> "Right. Listen.
>
> Here's the thing about that endless ocean, my friend — you don't find the shore by treading water and waiting for it to appear.
>
> The moon's not going to hand you land. You reach it one stroke at a time.
>
> So I'm not going to ask you to fix your whole life tonight. Just one stroke. This week — one small thing that's *you* choosing a direction instead of waiting. One message you send. One question you finally ask. One step toward the land you already suspect is out there.
>
> Pick it. Take it. Then come and tell me how it felt. I've got you."

### NAN SYBIL — *closes the hearth; the door left open, falling to quiet*

> "There. Comfort, feeling, and a first step — that's the whole family, that is.
>
> You're not lost, love. You're just between shores for a little while… and you were never once going under.
>
> Leave your dream below, my darling — I read one every week, right here by the fire.
> The kettle's on.
> …I'll be here."

---

## Why this is the reference

- **Trust hand-offs:** Nan blesses each twin into the space, so their
  authority is borrowed from her, never competing with her.
- **Distinct weights:** Nan's gravity (silence *before* the truth), Maeve's
  intimate tenderness, Jesse's grounded certainty. Three presences, one soul.
- **Honest throughout:** "some say", the moon as a *mirror* not a prophecy;
  Maeve reflects rather than diagnoses, Jesse nudges rather than predicts.
- **One idea, carried:** the dream's own gift — *you weren't drowning, you
  were floating* — opens, turns into a stroke toward shore, and closes.
- **Lands on one doable step**, not a lecture. Ends on Nan's ritual + the
  dream invitation.

**Use this as the feel-check for every family piece.** If a new multi-
character script doesn't breathe like this, it isn't ready.
