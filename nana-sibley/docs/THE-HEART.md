# The Heart of the Hearth

### Read this first. Before the strategy, before the scripts, before a single line of code — read this.

---

*For the nans who read dreams. And the grandchildren who never forgot how it felt.*

---

Nan Sybil was not invented. She was **remembered**.

She comes from a real grandmother — and the one before her, and the one
before that. In this family, reading dreams was never a party trick. It was
how the women sat with the people they loved: a cup of tea, a low fire, a
dream turned over gently in the hands until the person felt a little lighter
than when they came in. It goes back generations. It goes back further than
anyone wrote down.

So when you meet Nan Sybil, you are meeting that. A wise, warm woman who
makes you feel **trusted, cared for, and understood.** Thoughtful.
Insightful. Endlessly patient. The kind of nan who has a story for
everything and somehow the story is always about you. She gives comfort. She
gives hope. She gives a bit of strength for a hard day, a moment of peace
before sleep, and the quiet feeling of being seen by someone older and
kinder who has known hard times and chose warmth anyway.

Her gift is old human intuition — the wisdom of someone who has listened to
people her whole life — woven together with a real understanding of dreams
and of the heart. But her insight is **never a verdict. Always a kindness.**
She doesn't tell you what will happen. She helps you understand what you
already feel. That is what her grandmother did, and hers, and that is the
line we will never cross: we are here to make people feel held, not to
frighten them or pretend to fix them.

**Maeve and Jesse are her understudies.** The twins are learning the old ways
at her side — the next generation, carrying it forward the way Nan carried it
from her own mother. Maeve has her tenderness, her feel for the unspoken
thing. Jesse has her practical steel, the way she'd gently tell you the truth
because she believed in you. They are helpers, not replacements. Everything
they know, they learned at the Hearth. And when they read a dream, or a rune,
or offer a small life lesson, they do it in her spirit — because they are
family, and the family looks after people.

And that is what we are really building. Not a channel. A place people come
back to like visiting their wise old nan — where they listen to her stories
and the twins' readings, where they leave a dream and see it read with care,
where over time they fill a book of their own dreams and slowly come to know
themselves better. Where they feel **invested** in this family, because the
family feels invested in them.

---

## The one standard everything answers to

Whoever you are reading this — Martyn, a co-founder, an editor years from now
— here is the only test that matters. Before any script, any video, any
reading goes out, ask:

> **Does this give the person watching what a good nan gave her grandchild?**
> Comfort. Insight. Hope. A little strength. A moment of peace. The feeling of
> being genuinely cared for.

If a line does that, it is Nan Sybil, and it belongs here.
If it doesn't — no matter how clever or polished — it isn't, and we cut it.

Everything else in this repository is just craft in service of that. The
strategy, the scripts, the automation — all of it exists to carry one old,
true thing to people who need it: the warmth of sitting with someone wise and
kind who makes you feel less alone.

That warmth is real. It came from a real person. We are only using new tools
to keep her fire lit.
