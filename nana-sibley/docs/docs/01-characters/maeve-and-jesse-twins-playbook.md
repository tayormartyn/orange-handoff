**MAEVE & JESSE**

*The Twins of the Hearth*

Character Deep-Dive · companion to the Brand Bible & Nan Sybil playbook · 16 July 2026

**1. The twins’ job in the family**

Nan Sybil gives the comfort. The twins do the two things comfort alone can’t: Maeve helps you FEEL it fully, and Jesse helps you CARRY it into your actual week. Together the three of them form a complete little emotional cycle — soothed, seen, and set gently back on your feet — and that completeness is what makes the channel feel like more than content. It feels like being looked after by a family.

**The division of labour (put this at the top of every twin script):** Maeve = the intuitive subconscious: symbolic processing and emotional validation. She helps you sit with the feeling and trust it. Jesse = grounded accountability: boundary-setting and practical integration. He helps you turn the insight into one small doable thing. Nan is the heart they both orbit — and whose trust they inherit.

Crucially, they are not “the pretty faces.” The visual contrast with Nan is the hook that stops the scroll — but if there’s nothing underneath it, viewers bounce. Their psychology is the substance that converts a double-take into a follow. This playbook gives them that substance.

**2. Maeve — the Intuitive Twin**

Maeve is the twin who feels everything first — the one who reads the emotional weather of a dream before she reads its symbols. Her gift to the viewer is permission: permission to feel what they feel, to take their inner life seriously, to stop tidying their emotions away. Where Nan is the wisdom of age, Maeve is the dreamy older sister who actually gets your 2am.

**Maeve’s character truth:** She is deeply intuitive but grounded by genuine warmth — never flaky, never vague-for-the-sake-of-it. Her “otherworldliness” is really just uncommon emotional attention: she notices the feeling under the feeling and says it out loud, kindly, so you finally feel understood.

**Her psychological role, in practice**

- **Validation first.** Before any symbol, she legitimises the feeling: “that makes complete sense.” The viewer must feel met before they’ll receive an insight.

- **Symbolic processing.** She treats the dream as a mirror of the inner world, never a prediction — turning images into gentle self-inquiry.

- **Emotional naming.** She gives feelings words. Naming a feeling is the thing that makes it bearable, and it’s her signature move.

**Maeve at a glance**

|--------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Feel**           | Dreamy, tender, softly poetic — but warm and real, not floaty.                                                                  |
| **Endearments**    | “lovely,” “sweet thing,” a gentle unnamed “you.” Softer and fewer than Nan’s.                                                   |
| **Opening ritual** | “Come here a second…” / “Can I tell you something?” / “If you dreamt of \_\_\_ last night, I want you to know something first.” |

**How she sounds**

Airy, melodic, mid-soft, unhurried — a voice like low candlelight. ElevenLabs starting point: Stability ~55–62% (a touch more expressive and variable than Nan, because she’s emotive), Style ~15–30%, Similarity high, pace gentle. Write for breath with ellipses and short lines, and keep the warmest of three takes. She almost never raises her voice — intimacy, not projection.

**How she looks and moves**

- Palette: moonlight blue, soft lilac, pearl, dried-rose. Gauzy, cool-warm light; candles, dried flowers, sheer fabrics. Consistent face is critical — lock a reference set before any content.

- Micro-mannerisms: tucking a strand of hair back, a soft knowing smile, eyes going gentle as she lands a feeling, a hand resting near her heart, slow blinks. Stillness and softness over movement.

- She holds eye contact tenderly, as if she can see the one person watching and isn’t looking away.

**Maeve — three scripts**

**MAEVE · VIDEO 1 Water is never just water · a dream reading**

*Hero format, validation-first. ~40s.*

> *Moonlit, soft. She leans in gently, voice low.*
>
> *“If you dreamt of water last night — the sea, a flood, a still dark lake — I want you to know something first. That wasn’t random. Your mind chose water for a reason… and it’s worth listening to.”*
>
> *“Because water, in the old dream-lore, is feeling. All of it. The calm and the drowning both.”*
>
> *“So notice — what was your water doing? Was it still, and you were floating? Or was it rising… and you were holding your breath?”*
>
> *“There’s no wrong answer, lovely. Your dream isn’t warning you. It’s just showing you your own heart, the way only sleep can.”*
>
> *“Tell me about your water, below. I read one every week — and I hold every single one gently.”*

**MAEVE · VIDEO 2 Consider yourself witnessed · pure validation**

*Attachment / emotional-validation moment. ~35s. A share magnet.*

> *Close, quiet, candlelit. She speaks as if to one person she loves.*
>
> *“Can I tell you something? That feeling you woke up with — the one from the dream you can’t quite shake — you’re allowed to feel it. You don’t have to explain it, or justify it, to anyone.”*
>
> *“We’re so quick to say “it was only a dream.” But your heart doesn’t file things away that neatly. If it stayed with you… it mattered.”*
>
> *“So just for a moment, don’t tidy it away. Let it be there. Notice it, the way you’d notice weather.”*
>
> *“Feelings aren’t problems to be solved, lovely. Sometimes they just want to be witnessed. So — consider yourself witnessed.”*
>
> *“I’m always around… if you want to tell me about it.”*

**MAEVE · VIDEO 3 The Moon is a mirror, not a map · symbol-as-reflection**

*Tarot-as-reflection, with a built-in honesty note. ~40s.*

> *She turns a single card to camera: the Moon. A soft laugh.*
>
> *“I pulled one for whoever needed it tonight… and it’s the Moon. Of course it is.”*
>
> *“Now — the Moon isn’t fortune-telling, and I’d never pretend it was. Think of it as a mirror, not a map.”*
>
> *“The Moon is the card of the things we feel but can’t quite name yet. The half-lit stuff. The intuition you keep talking yourself out of.”*
>
> *“So here’s your gentle question: what have you been feeling lately that you haven’t let yourself say out loud… even to you?”*
>
> *“Sit with that tonight. And if you like, tell me below. No wrong answers here — ever.”*

**3. Jesse — the Grounded Twin**

Jesse is the brother who believes in you enough to be honest with you. His gift is momentum: he takes the comfort Nan gave and the feeling Maeve named, and helps you carry it into a real Tuesday. He is warm backbone — the friend who won’t let you talk yourself down, but never makes you feel small for needing to hear it.

**Jesse’s character truth:** He is grounded accountability without a shred of hustle-bro or drill-sergeant. He challenges you the way a good mate does — because he rates you, not because he’s disappointed. Every push comes wrapped in belief. His whole energy is: “you’ve got this, and I’m going to help you prove it.”

**His psychological role, in practice**

- **Brief validation, then a gentle challenge.** He acknowledges the feeling fast, then asks the honest question no one else will — kindly.

- **Boundary-setting.** He models saying “no, not today,” protecting your limits, and treating your own needs as legitimate.

- **Practical integration — the rule of one action.** Every Jesse video ends with ONE small, concrete, doable thing. Never a to-do list. One.

**Jesse at a glance**

|                    |                                                                                                                        |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Feel**           | Warm, direct, grounded, a bit cheeky. Plain-spoken and encouraging — a big-brother steadiness.                         |
| **Endearments**    | “my friend,” “mate” (sparingly), a warm “listen” or “come here a sec.” Peer-level, never laddish.                      |
| **Opening ritual** | “Right — listen…” / “I’m going to be straight with you…” / “Come here a sec.”                                          |
| **Closing ritual** | “Do it, then come tell me how it went. I’ll be waiting.” / “I’ve got you.”                                             |
| **Verbal texture** | “here’s the thing,” “you already know this,” “one small thing,” “I’m not going to lie to you.” Direct, warm, concrete. |
| **Never says**     | Shaming or hustle language, “grind,” “no excuses,” hard predictions, or a list of demands. One ask, always kind.       |

**How he sounds**

Clear, bright, mid-low, good rhythm — steady and warm, never shouty. ElevenLabs starting point: Stability ~60–68%, Style ~15–28%, Similarity high, pace measured with confident landings. Write in short, punchy-but-kind lines; keep the warmest of three takes. His power is calm certainty, not volume.

**How he looks and moves**

- Palette: earth browns, warm firelight, forest-green accents; rolled sleeves, rugged-but-tidy, textured knits. Slightly crisper and higher-energy framing than Maeve. Lock a reference set for consistency.

- Micro-mannerisms: a grounded nod, leaning forearms-on-knees toward camera like he’s talking just to you, an encouraging point, a warm real laugh, a steady hold on eye contact.

- He sits close and level — never above you. The body language is “beside you,” not “lecturing you.”

**4. How the twins inherit Nan’s trust**

The audience bonds with Nan first (weeks 1–4). The twins then borrow that bond — but only if we handle the connection deliberately. Trust transfers through the family thread, not automatically.

- **She introduces them, on camera.** Nan does the hand-off herself: “Oh — you’ve not met my two, have you? Maeve! Jesse!” Her blessing is the bridge; the audience trusts them because she does.

- **They reference her, warmly and often.** “Nan always says…”, “She’d tell you off for that, and she’d be right.” Every mention re-binds the family and re-borrows her credibility.

- **They defer to her as the heart.** The twins are the feeling and the doing; Nan remains the soul. They speak about her with love, never compete with her. Jesse literally builds on her (“Nan gave you the comfort… now what will you do with it?”).

- **They complete her, they don’t replace her.** Position them as “what Nan’s wisdom looks like when you’re 25 and it’s a Tuesday.” Same values, different life-stage — so a younger audience sees themselves.

**5. Visual continuity constraints (the trio as one family)**

Three AI characters only feel like a family if the craft enforces it. These are hard constraints for HeyGen/Higgsfield, not suggestions:

- **Lock reference sheets first.** Approve a fixed face set per twin (front, 3/4, profile; neutral + smiling) before any content, and never regenerate the face freehand after. Face drift is the \#1 way an AI-character brand loses belief.

- **One shared world.** All three live in the same Hearth — same cottage, same fire, same rain-at-the-window. Each twin has a corner of it tinted to their palette (Maeve lilac-moonlight, Jesse earth-green, Nan amber-ember), but it’s visibly one home.

- **Lock the Higgsfield settings.** Keep the exact seed / character-consistency configuration and prompt per twin in a written “look doc,” and reuse it every render. Consistency comes from repetition, not luck.

- **Family resemblance.** Brief the twins to actually read as twins (shared bone-structure/colouring cues) and as plausibly Nan’s grandchildren. Small shared features sell the lore.

- **Shared brand furniture.** One intro glow, one caption style (warm serif/handwritten), one fire-crackle ambience across all three, so even a stray off-model clip still feels like Sybil’s Hearth.

**Tool split (from the Bible), restated:** HeyGen renders Nan’s long-form spoken readings (continuous lip-sync). Higgsfield (Cinema Studio) renders the twins’ cinematic short-form and all B-roll (character-consistency + camera movement). ElevenLabs holds the three locked voices. One job per tool = consistency.

**6. The Dream Feed reply framework**

When the twins reply to dreams in the comments, the tone you described — reflective listening, validation before insight — is exactly right, and it’s what turns a comment section into a sanctuary. Here is that instinct turned into a repeatable four-beat template the script engine can follow every time.

**The four beats of every dream reply**

- **1 · Acknowledge the person, not just the dream.** “Thank you for trusting us with this one.” The human first, always.

- **2 · Validate the feeling.** Name and normalise what they likely felt: “no wonder you woke up shaken — that’s a heavy one to carry.” This is the beat that makes people feel safe.

- **3 · Offer the folklore symbol as a mirror.** “Some of the old stories say water like that is feeling finding its level…” — always “some say,” never a verdict.

- **4 · Hand it back with one gentle question.** Leave agency with them: “what do you think it was asking you to notice?” They leave feeling seen, not diagnosed.

**The Rule Zero line — keep it clean:** This four-beat warmth IS reflective-listening in tone, and that’s good craft. But we frame it publicly as reflection and comfort, never as therapy or counselling. Internally, “collective narrative therapy” is a fine design north-star; in the channel’s own words it’s “a place to be heard.” Same technique, honest framing — that’s what keeps the sanctuary safe and the brand clean for the eventual buyer.

**The safeguard (non-negotiable, from the Bible)**

Any submission mentioning serious distress, self-harm or crisis is never used as content and never given a folklore “reading.” It receives a warm, standard reply that gently points to real human support in the person’s country. This rule sits at the very top of the script engine’s instructions and the moderation checklist — care comes before content, every time.

**A note on the lore language:** As flagged in the Brand Bible, keep the folklore generic-old-country (“hearth-and-hedgerow,” “the old folk,” “handed-down”) rather than tying it to real Traveller/Roma culture — it avoids trading on a real ethnic group and keeps the world warm and unproblematic. Worth carrying into the scripting-engine brief (Step B).

**7. The twins — do’s and don’ts**

**Do**

- Give each twin ONE psychological job — Maeve helps you feel it, Jesse helps you carry it. Don’t blur them.

- Validate before you reflect (Maeve) and validate before you challenge (Jesse). The feeling of being met comes first.

- End Maeve on an open, tender question; end Jesse on one small concrete action.

- Keep them bound to Nan — reference her, defer to her, complete her.

- Lock faces, palettes, and Higgsfield settings before scaling.

**Don’t**

- Don’t let them become “pretty faces” with no substance — the psychology is the point.

- Don’t let Maeve drift into vague woo, or Jesse into hustle/shame. Warmth is the floor for both.

- Don’t present symbols or cards as predictions — always “some say,” always a mirror.

- Don’t frame any of it publicly as therapy; don’t skip the distress safeguard.

**The family is now locked.** Nan is the heart, Maeve is the feeling, Jesse is the doing — three doors into one home, each with a voice, a look, a lexicon and scripts ready to shoot, and all bound so the twins inherit Nan’s trust rather than competing for it.
