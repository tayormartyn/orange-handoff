**SYBIL’S HEARTH**

**NAN SYBIL**

*The Voice of the Hearth*

*Character truth · her lexicon · how she sounds · how she moves · the first five scripts*

Character Deep-Dive · companion to the Brand Bible · 16 July 2026

**1. Who Nan Sybil really is**

Before a single line is written, we have to agree on the one thing that makes her work — because it’s the opposite of what most people assume. A lovable granny is not a sweet granny. Sweetness alone is forgettable, and worse, it reads as fake. What makes Nan Sybil someone people fall in love with is that her warmth is earned.

**Her character truth (put this at the top of every script brief):** Nan Sybil has lived a long, not-always-easy life — and she chose warmth anyway. She’s seen loss, she’s known hard winters, and that’s exactly why her comfort lands: it’s not naïve, it’s hard-won. She isn’t sweet because she doesn’t know any better. She’s kind because she knows exactly how much people need it.

That gives her three layers, and the magic is having all three at once:

- **Warmth —** the cosy, welcoming, “come in from the cold” heart of her. Terms of endearment, the fire, the kettle, the open door.

- **Wisdom —** a quiet depth. She’s seen things. She doesn’t panic, doesn’t rush, and she says the true thing gently. This is where your real counselling ear lives.

- **A little mischief —** a dry, cheeky twinkle. A soft laugh at herself, a knowing look. This is the ingredient that stops her being a greetings-card and makes her feel like a real person you’d want to sit with.

**The test for every line she says:** does it sound like a real, warm, slightly weathered grandmother — or does it sound like an AI being “nice”? If it’s the second, cut it. Specificity beats sweetness every time: “drink your tea while it’s warm” lands; “I care about your wellbeing” is death.

**2. The warmth formula — how she makes people feel loved**

Lovability isn’t vague. It’s a set of repeatable moves. Nan does these, on purpose, in almost every video:

- **She notices you.** “Oh, there you are.” “You’ve had one of those days, haven’t you.” She speaks as if she can see the one person watching — because on a phone, at night, she can.

- **She invites, never demands.** “Come and sit by me a minute.” “You don’t have to say a word.” She lowers the cost of being with her to zero.

- **She gives permission.** “You don’t have to carry all of it tonight.” People are exhausted by their own standards; she lets them off the hook. This is the single most shared kind of moment.

- **She’s specific and sensory.** Tea, fire, rain, a shawl, a biscuit. Small physical things make her real and make the viewer feel held.

- **She believes in you.** “You’ve done ever so well to get this far.” Quiet, direct pride — the thing people most want to hear and least often do.

- **She ends soft.** Never a hard sell, never a cliff. A little blessing, and the door left open: “I’ll be here.”

**The rule of one:** One person, one feeling, one small piece of comfort per video. Don’t make her do three things. A single, well-landed “you did well to get through today, love” is worth more than a clever monologue. Depth beats breadth — every time.

**3. Her lexicon — the exact words she uses**

Consistency of language is what turns a character into someone people know. Nan needs a fixed vocabulary of endearments, openers, and closers that fans start to anticipate and love.

**Terms of endearment (her signatures)**

Primary, used most: love, my darling, pet. Secondary, for variety: my lovely, sweetheart, petal, duck. Use one per beat, not stacked — “Come here, love” not “Come here, love, my darling, sweetheart.”

**Pick ONE regional anchor and keep it:** Warmth reads as fake when the accent wanders. Choose a single region and stay there. Good options: soft Yorkshire/Northern (“love,” “pet,” “duck,” “ey up” — warm, widely loved, very “nan”); West Country (“my lover,” “my bird” — cosy but strong); or gentle generic-Southern (“darling,” “my dear”). Recommendation: soft Northern English — it’s the most universally read as “the loveliest nan.” Lock it and brief the voice to match.

**Her opening ritual (start most videos with a version)**

- “Oh — there you are.”

- “Come in, come in, you’re letting the cold in.”

- “Come and sit by me a minute, love.”

**Her closing ritual (end EVERY video the same way)**

- “The kettle’s on. I’ll be here.”

- “I’m always here, by the fire.”

- “Leave me your dream below, love — I read one every week.”

**Her little verbal tics (sprinkle, don’t overdo)**

“…isn’t it,” “…you know,” “ever so,” “now then,” “bless you,” “go on,” a soft self-correcting laugh (“oh, listen to me”). These fillers are what make speech sound human rather than scripted. Two or three per video, no more.

**Words to ban from her mouth:** wellbeing, journey, self-care, energy (as in “your energy”), content, subscribe, guys, hey. Corporate or influencer words shatter the spell instantly. She’s a grandmother, not a brand.

**4. How she sounds (voice direction)**

The voice is half the love. A warm face with a slightly-off voice is worse than nothing. Direction for ElevenLabs (or your chosen voice engine):

**The target**

Low-ish, soft-edged, unhurried, with a faint smile in it and the gentle grain of age. She sounds like she’s sitting close and speaking just to you — never projecting, never performing.

**Settings to start from**

- **Stability ~65–72%:** consistent enough to sound like the same nan every time, but not so high it goes flat and robotic. Too much stability is the \#1 cause of “dead” AI grandmother voices.

- **Similarity high:** once you pick or clone the voice, keep it locked so she never drifts.

- **Style/exaggeration low (~10–25%):** warmth comes from restraint, not performance. High style makes her theatrical and fake.

- **Pace slow:** she is never in a hurry. Silence is her superpower.

**Write for the breath (this matters more than the settings)**

The voice engine reads your punctuation as breathing. Write her scripts the way she’d actually speak them — with the pauses built in:

> *Flat (wrong): “There that’s better isn’t it you found your way to the Hearth.”*
>
> *Breathing (right): “There. That’s better… isn’t it? You found your way to the Hearth.”*

Full stops for landings, ellipses for the soft trailing pauses, commas for little breaths, one thought per line. Read every script aloud yourself before rendering — if you run out of breath, so will she.

**The three-take rule:** Render every line 2–3 times and keep the warmest, not the first. AI voices vary between generations; the warmest take of three is noticeably lovelier than a single pass. Build this into production — it’s the cheapest quality upgrade you have.

**5. How she looks and moves (beating the uncanny valley)**

This is where most AI-avatar channels lose people: a technically-perfect face that feels dead. The cure is small human imperfections and warm, low light. Brief HeyGen and your editor for these.

**The setting & light**

- Firelight and lamplight only — warm amber, soft shadows, never flat or clinical white light. The glow is the hug.

- Rain or dark at the window, so the inside feels safe by contrast. A fire, a worn armchair, old books, a teacup, knitting. No modern objects, ever.

- Slightly soft focus. A touch of warmth and grain reads as cosy and, helpfully, hides the AI “sheen.”

**The micro-mannerisms (the secret to “she’s alive”)**

- A small, real smile that reaches the eyes — not a fixed grin. Brief for “gentle, closed-mouth smile, eyes soft.”

- Little human beats: a soft laugh, looking down then back up, a slow blink, a small head-tilt, adjusting her shawl, both hands around a warm cup.

- Direct, soft eye contact — she’s talking to ONE person, holding their gaze like family would.

- Stillness. Let her hold a beat without cutting. Warmth needs room; fast cuts make her feel nervous and fake.

**On screen**

- Captions in a warm serif or soft handwritten style (never a hard tech sans-serif), so even muted she feels hand-made.

- A gentle, consistent intro glow and the same soft fire-crackle ambience every video — sound is half the cosiness.

**The honest-AI note stays:** None of this warmth requires pretending she’s a living person — the channel is labelled AI (Brand Bible, Section 1), and the About/pinned note says so plainly. A beloved character can be openly crafted; audiences adore openly-fictional characters. Warmth and honesty are not in tension here.

**7. When (and how) to reveal the twins**

Your instinct on the contrast is right — the visual jolt of the two striking twins against cosy Nan is a genuine hook. But timing is everything: reveal them too early and you dilute Nan before people have bonded with her; too late and you plateau. The sequence:

- **Weeks 1–4: Nan solo.** Let people fall completely for her first. She is the emotional anchor and the trust. Don’t split focus.

- **The hand-off reveal.** Once Nan has a loyal core commenting every day, SHE introduces them — that keeps the family thread and lends them her trust: “Oh — you’ve not met my two, have you? Maeve! Jesse! Come and say hello.” Cut to the twins. The contrast does the work.

- **Then run the trio.** Nan keeps the long-form fireside heart; the twins carry the high-velocity, visually-striking short-form (per the Growth Engine doc). The audience now has three doors in, all bound by the family.

**Keep her the heart:** Even once the twins are popular, Nan stays the soul of the brand and the trust anchor — she’s the one who’ll eventually carry the premium fireside videos. Never let the twins’ flash eclipse her warmth; the warmth is the moat.

**8. Nan Sybil — do’s and don’ts**

**Do**

- Give her the weathered depth under the warmth — kind because she’s known hard times, not because she’s naïve.

- Keep one region, one set of endearments, the same open and close, every time.

- Let her be specific and sensory — tea, fire, rain, a biscuit — and let her be still.

- End every video soft, with the ritual and the dream invitation.

- Read every script aloud before rendering, and keep the warmest of three takes.

**Don’t**

- Don’t make her saccharine or over-sweet — it reads as fake and forgettable.

- Don’t let her lecture, over-explain, or say more than one comforting thing per video.

- Don’t use influencer/corporate words (wellbeing, journey, subscribe, guys).

- Don’t rush her, over-cut her, or light her with flat white light.

- Don’t claim she’s a real living person — the channel is openly AI, and she’s adored anyway.
