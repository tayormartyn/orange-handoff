**1. The Foundation: Honest-AI, by design**

**The rule:** Sybil’s Hearth never claims its characters are real living people. Nan Sybil, Maeve and Jesse are presented, openly, as AI-crafted characters in a storytelling world — with a real person (you) behind the readings and the care.

This is worth doing properly because it is the difference between a business that scales and one that gets deleted. Three reasons, in the order that matters to you:

**It is now platform law, not opinion**

As of 2026, both major platforms you’re building on require disclosure of exactly the kind of content you’re making — photorealistic AI-generated people who appear to speak:

- **YouTube** requires creators to disclose when they use AI to “meaningfully alter or generate photorealistic content,” including realistic AI-generated people saying things. It is set per-upload in YouTube Studio. Non-disclosure can lead to forced labelling, content removal, or suspension from the Partner Program — i.e. demonetisation.

- **TikTok** requires labelling of realistic AI-generated images, audio or video, is rolling out automatic detection labels, and names its AI effects explicitly. Undisclosed realistic AI content is a Community Guidelines issue.

**The good news:** a disclosed AI character is completely allowed and thriving on both platforms. AI creators are a recognised, monetisable category. Disclosure does not cap your growth — deception caps your growth, because it ends in a strike.

**It is what makes the asset sellable**

You said the end-goal is a private-equity-grade IP asset that runs without your face. A buyer runs diligence. “The brand secretly told a vulnerable audience that a fake dying grandmother was a real woman” is an un-buyable liability — it exposes the buyer to platform bans, refund liability, and reputational blow-up. “A beloved, clearly-labelled AI storytelling brand with a real practitioner behind it” is a clean asset. Honesty is the moat around your exit.

**It is a better story anyway**

You don’t lose the magic — you upgrade it. “We built a warm, hand-crafted AI family to carry old folklore and dream-lore to a new generation, and there’s a real person reading every dream” is a 2026 story people root for. It lets you show the craft, bring your own face and counselling background in as the trust anchor, and never look over your shoulder. Your instinct to “reveal the real person behind it” is right — we just lead with it instead of hiding it.

**One-line public framing:** “Sybil’s Hearth is an AI-crafted storytelling world of folklore, dreams and reflection — with a real person behind every reading.” Put a version of this in every bio, About page, and channel description.

**2. Positioning & Promise**

**What we are**

A warm, cinematic folklore-and-dreams brand: an AI-crafted family who tell old stories, read dreams for reflection, and offer a nightly moment of calm. Comfort content with production values people don’t expect at this price point.

**What we are not**

Not therapy, not counselling, not medical or mental-health treatment, and not a claim to replace a professional. This isn’t timidity — it’s the line that keeps the brand legal and safe (full guardrails in Section 8). We sell reflection and entertainment, beautifully made.

**The audience**

People who find comfort in the spiritual/wellness/folklore space — the same audience that watches sleep stories, tarot, cosy mythology and “dream meaning” content. They come for warmth, ritual and a sense of being seen. They stay for consistency and craft.

**The one-sentence promise**

**Promise:** Every night, the Hearth is lit and someone is glad you came — a story, a dream, a moment of calm before you sleep.

**3. The World & The Lore**

The lore is presented as an openly-fictional world — the way an audience happily accepts a beloved animated family. Keeping it fictional-by-consent is what lets it stay warm and un-cynical.

**The Hearth.** A place out of time — a stone cottage, a fire that never quite goes out, rain on the window, a kettle always warm. Three voices keep it lit and pass old wisdom to whoever pulls up a chair. It is deliberately un-modern: candlelight, worn books, hand-written journals.

**The family.** Nan Sybil is the keeper of the Hearth. The twins, Maeve and Jesse, are the younger generation carrying her stories outward. The emotional engine of the brand is simple and true: a grandmother’s lifetime of stories and dream-lore being lovingly preserved and shared before it’s lost.

**The honest layer.** Woven through, never hidden: this world is AI-crafted, and a real person reads the dreams. The channel trailer and About page tell that story with pride — the craft is part of the appeal, not a secret to protect.

**Traveller wisdom — handle with care:** The original brief leaned on “traditional traveller wisdom.” Drawing on real Romani/Traveller culture as set-dressing risks trading on an ethnic stereotype and can read as appropriation. Recommend keeping the flavour generic-folkloric — “old-country,” “hearth-and-hedgerow,” “handed-down” — rather than tying it to a real, marginalised ethnic group.

**5. Brand Voice & Tone**

Across all three characters and every caption, the Hearth sounds the same underneath: warm, unhurried, respectful, never hypey. We speak to one person, at night, kindly.

**We are**

- Warm — like being welcomed in from the cold.

- Calm — slow sentences, room to breathe, no urgency.

- Sincere — we mean it; we never wink at the audience or talk down to them.

- Grounded — wonder and comfort, but honest; we don’t promise magic.

**We are not**

- Clickbaity, fear-based, or “you won’t BELIEVE” energy.

- Salesy — the product is barely mentioned until trust is earned.

- Clinical or preachy — no diagnosis language, no lectures.

**Words we use / avoid**

**Use:** reflection, comfort, wonder, story, dream, symbol, calm, hearth, gently, perhaps, some say.

**Avoid:** therapy, counselling, diagnosis, cure, guaranteed, will happen, proven, treatment, patient. (These are the words that turn entertainment into a regulated claim — see Section 8.)

**6. Visual Consistency System**

The number-one failure mode of AI-character brands is drift — the face subtly changes between videos and the audience stops believing in the character. Prevent it with a locked reference system before you scale.

**The colour & light world**

- **Nan Sybil:** amber, ember-red, candle-gold, deep shadow. Firelight always present.

- **Maeve:** moonlight blue, soft lilac, pearl, dried-rose. Gauzy, cool-warm glow.

- **Jesse:** earth browns, warm firelight, forest green accents. Slightly crisper, higher energy.

- Shared: candle glow, worn wood, old books, rain-on-glass, no modern tech ever in frame.

**The lock-it-down checklist**

- Generate and approve a reference sheet per character (front, 3/4, profile; neutral + smiling) before making content. Save these as the canonical face.

- Reuse the same seed/character-consistency feature in Higgsfield for the twins on every render; keep a written note of the exact prompt + settings that produced the approved look.

- Keep one “look doc” per character: wardrobe, hair, setting, lighting, camera feel — paste it into every generation prompt.

- Use HeyGen for Nan’s talking-head continuity; keep her avatar fixed once approved rather than regenerating.

- Build a shared intro/outro, lower-third style, font, and logo mark so even off-model clips feel like one brand.

**Tool split, in one line:** HeyGen = mouths moving for real (Nan’s spoken readings). Higgsfield = cinematic beauty and motion (the twins’ shorts and all B-roll). ElevenLabs = the three locked voices. Don’t mix these lanes — consistency comes from each tool doing the one job it’s best at.

**7. Content Pillars & the Signature Loop**

Four repeatable content types, so the algorithm and the audience always know what they’re getting. Pick one hero format and make twenty before diversifying.

**Pillar 1 — Dream of the Week (the signature loop)**

Your strongest single idea. Viewers leave a dream in the comments; one is chosen and gently reflected on-screen (Maeve for short-form, Nan for long-form). It manufactures comment velocity — the exact signal the algorithm rewards — and gives people a reason to return. This is the top-of-funnel engine; build the brand around it.

**Pillar 2 — Fireside Tales**

Nan Sybil, long-form (5–8 min), telling an atmospheric folk story or offering a slow, comforting reflection. This is the deep-connection, “fall asleep to this” content. HeyGen home turf.

**Pillar 3 — Symbols & Signs**

Maeve and Jesse, short-form: “what it means when you dream of water / falling / a door,” tarot-as-reflection, rune/palm symbolism as metaphor. Beautiful, fast, shareable. Pure discovery fuel.

**Pillar 4 — A Moment of Calm**

Ultra-short ritual content: a candle, a breath, a single line of comfort. Cheap to make, strong for saves and shares, reinforces the nightly-habit promise.

**8. Guardrails: what keeps this safe & legal**

These are not optional polish — they are the rails that keep the brand out of two specific ditches: platform strikes (Section 1) and mental-health/consumer-protection claims. Put them in every scriptwriter brief and every prompt.

**The two hard lines**

- **Always disclosed as AI.** Every channel bio, About page, and (where the platform requires it) upload is labelled as AI-generated. No character ever claims to be a living human.

- **Reflection, never treatment.** We never present dream readings as therapy, counselling, diagnosis, or a substitute for professional care — no matter how good the writing is. The value we sell is comfort, story and self-insight.

**Standard footer line (use on readings & the site)**

**Disclaimer:** Sybil’s Hearth is AI-crafted storytelling and reflective entertainment. Our readings are for comfort and self-reflection and are not therapy, counselling, medical or psychological advice. If you’re struggling, please reach out to a qualified professional or a support line in your country.

**On “better than a human counsellor”**

This claim can’t appear anywhere — marketing or private belief. It’s false (AI doesn’t replace licensed care), it’s the exact phrasing that attracts regulatory attention, and some people who submit raw dreams will be in genuine distress. Your real counselling background is a wonderful trust asset — framed honestly as “a real person who cares reads these,” never as “this beats a therapist.”

**Vulnerable-audience care**

- Have a simple rule for dream submissions that mention serious distress or self-harm: those are never used for content, and the standard response points warmly to real support.

- Keep readings gentle and non-deterministic — “some say,” “perhaps,” “you might reflect on” — never “this means your marriage will end.”
