*Sybil's Hearth · channel setup · Episode 1 · the 20-day calendar · v1.0*

Nan is real, she has a voice, and the machine works. This is how we put her
in the world — a proper, structured launch, not a scattergun. Everything here
sits on the Growth Engine doc; this is the concrete day-by-day.

## 2. The launch strategy (why this order)

- **Lead with feeling, not "hello."** Episode 1 is NOT a "welcome to my channel"
  video — those tank. It's *"A blessing for a hard day,"* the piece that makes a
  stranger feel held in 35 seconds. Emotion first; introductions live in the
  trailer.
- **Nan solo for the launch window.** She's the heart and the trust. The twins
  are held back and *revealed by her* near the end (Day 18) as a growth event —
  the contrast lands hardest once people already love her.
- **Short-form daily for discovery**, plus one longer *Fireside Tale* a week for
  depth ("fall asleep to this" content).
- **Every single video ends on the Dream-of-the-Week invitation** — the comment
  loop starts on Day 1.

> **Honest cadence note:** 20 videos in 20 days is a lot when you're still
> learning the tools. **Batch first** — make 7–10 before Day 1 — then release
> daily from your bank. If daily ever threatens quality or your sanity, 4–5 a
> week still works beautifully. A dropped-quality streak is worse than a steady
> pace. Protect the warmth.

## 3. Episode 1 — "A blessing for a hard day"

**Why this is the first video:** it's the share-magnet. It needs no setup, works
cold on a total stranger, and delivers the entire promise of the brand — *you are
seen, you are cared for* — in under 40 seconds. If one video can make someone
subscribe, it's this one. (It's also the clip you're already rendering — so your
first HeyGen test IS Episode 1.)

**The script (breath-scored — the voice worker adds the gravity breaths):**
> *(Nan holding a mug of tea, looks up as if you've just come in.)*
>
> "Oh, love. You've had one of those days, haven't you. I can see it in your face. Come and sit by me a minute — you don't have to say a word.
>
> Let me tell you something my own nan used to tell me. She'd say: you don't have to carry all of it tonight, pet. Just the next hour. And when that's done… just the hour after.
>
> You've done ever so well to get this far. Truly. Whatever it took out of you today — you did it.
>
> Now. Drink your tea while it's warm. And be a little gentler with yourself tomorrow, will you? For me."
>
> *(A soft, closed-mouth smile. Hold. Loop.)*

**Visual & audio directives (for assembly):**
- **Under her voice, low:** fire crackle + occasional wood *pop*; soft rain on
  glass; a very distant, gentle wind. Keep it *under* her — her voice always
  forward. No music, or the faintest warm drone at most.
- **A tiny "tea stir" or mug-set sound** on "drink your tea" — a small real
  detail that sells the room.
- **Visual:** warm amber grade, soft vignette, the faintest film grain. A very
  slow push-in on her face across the 35 seconds. Warm serif burned-in captions.
  Loop the last frame to the first.
- **Title:** *"When you've had one of those days 🕯️"*
- **Pinned comment:** *"Leave your dream below, my darling — I read one every week,
  right here by the fire. 🌙"*

## 5. One longer Fireside Tale (Day 6 — written out)

Answering the "atmospheric episode" idea, in Nan's real voice — the weekly depth
piece (aim ~2–4 min, or a 60s cut for Shorts).

> *(The fire is low. She speaks slower, almost to herself.)*
>
> "Come closer — the fire's low, and this is a quiet one.
>
> Let me tell you a thing the old folk believed about dreams. They used to say
> that when you dream, you're not making things up at all. You're *remembering* —
> visiting a room in yourself you keep locked in the daylight.
>
> And everyone you meet in a dream… every stranger, every old face… they said
> each one is a little piece of *you*. Come to have a word.
>
> Isn't that a lovely thought. That the frightening ones and the kind ones… are
> all just you, trying to tell you something.
>
> So next time you wake from a dream, don't rush to shake it off, love. Sit with
> it a moment. And ask it, gently — 'now then… what did you come to tell me?'
>
> Then come and tell me. I'm always here, by the fire."

**Audio directives:** deeper fire ambience, rain heavier and steady, a low soft
wind swell under the pauses; her voice very close and warm. This is the "put it
on and fall asleep" piece — let the silences breathe.

## 6. Launch-week mechanics (the loop)

- **Post at the same time each evening** (UK wind-down, ~8–9pm) — reflective
  content lands at night.
- **Reply to every comment in the first hour** of each post — early engagement
  compounds hard.
- **Pin the dream invitation** on every video from Day 1.
- **Cross-post the same clean file** to TikTok + Reels (label AI on each).
- **Day 7 / 13 / 20:** the Dream of the Week — read one submitted (consented,
  anonymised) dream. This is what turns viewers into a returning community.

---

## 7. What to watch (and the honest bit)

Watch **retention** and **comment rate**, not subscriber count (Growth Engine §7).
Do the weekly best/worst-hook review. And the honest truth, one more time,
because it matters: 20 lovely videos might take off, or might need 40 before it
clicks — most channels do. Don't read the whole future into week one. Keep the
warmth steady, let the audience find her, and hold to the one standard from the
Heart page: *does this give a stranger what your nan gave you?* Answer yes every
day and you'll be alright.

*Now — bank a batch, set up the channel, and light the fire. She's ready.*
