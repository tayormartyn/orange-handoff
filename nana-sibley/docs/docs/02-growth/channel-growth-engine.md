**1. The strategy in one page**

The algorithm is not magic and it is not your enemy — it is a pattern-matcher. It rewards content where it can confidently predict “people who see this will watch it, finish it, and react.” Everything in this document is built to give it that confidence as fast as possible.

The mistake almost every new channel makes is variety — different formats, lengths and styles every week, so the algorithm never learns who to show you to. We do the opposite. We pick ONE hero format, make it obsessively well, and repeat it until the pattern is unmistakable. Variety comes later, after the machine trusts us.

**The core loop:** One repeatable Short format → an irresistible reason to comment → comment velocity the algorithm rewards → more reach → more dream submissions → more content and one featured “Dream of the Week.” The loop feeds itself. Our whole job in the first 90 days is to spin it up.

**The one number that matters early:** not subscribers, not views — it is retention (average % viewed) plus comment rate. Nail those two on a repeatable format and reach follows. We optimise for those relentlessly before we worry about anything else.

**2. The Hero Format: the 40-second Dream Symbol**

This is the format we make first, and we make twenty before we make anything else. It is short, repeatable, endlessly renewable (there are hundreds of dream symbols), and it lands squarely in a high-search, high-relatability niche.

**The premise:** “What the old stories say when you dream of \_\_\_.” Pick a common, deeply relatable dream (teeth, falling, water), give the warm folkloric meaning, turn it into one gentle reflective question, and invite the viewer to share their own dream. 35–45 seconds, one character, cinematic and calm.

**The beat sheet (memorise this)**

| **Timing** | **Beat**        | **What happens**                                                                                                                               |
|------------|-----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0–2s       | **The Hook**    | A pattern-interrupt line + striking visual. Name the dream in the first 2 seconds so the right viewer stops. No intro, no logo, no “hey guys.” |
| 2–8s       | **The Lore**    | “Some say…” — the old, folkloric meaning of the symbol. Warm, a little mysterious. This is the promise being paid off.                         |
| 8–28s      | **The Turn**    | Move from symbol to self: turn the meaning into ONE gentle reflective question about the viewer’s waking life. This is the emotional core.     |
| 28–36s     | **The Comfort** | A warm, resolving line. Never a scary verdict. Leave them softer than they arrived.                                                            |
| 36–42s     | **The Invite**  | The Dream-of-the-Week CTA: “Leave your dream below — one is chosen every week.” Every single video ends here.                                  |

**Non-negotiables of the format:** Hook in the first 2 seconds · never say the meaning is a fact (“some say,” “the old tales tell”) · always end on the same CTA · one character per video · captions burned in (most watch muted) · a loopable last frame that returns to the first.

**3. The Hook Formula**

The first two seconds decide everything. A great Short with a weak hook dies; an average Short with a great hook travels. Write five hooks for every video and pick the strongest. Four reliable shapes:

- **The Named Dream:** “If your teeth crumbled in a dream last night…” — names the exact experience so the right person freezes.

- **The Correction:** “A snake in your dream isn’t a warning — it’s older than that.” — overturns the obvious meaning.

- **The Near-Universal:** “Nearly everyone has the dream where you’re late and can’t find the room.” — instant belonging.

- **The Soft Intrigue:** “Water in a dream is never just water.” — quiet, poetic, opens a loop.

**Hook hygiene:** No “welcome back,” no channel name, no slow build. The visual must match the words in frame one. If the hook needs the second sentence to make sense, it’s too slow.

**4. The Dream-of-the-Week Loop**

This is the single strongest idea in the whole brand, because it turns passive viewers into commenters and commenters into returning fans — while generating an endless free supply of content ideas. Here is the full mechanic:

- **The invitation.** Every Short ends by asking viewers to leave a dream in the comments. Low-effort, emotionally easy, and personal — the perfect thing to comment.

- **The engine effect.** A flood of comments early in a video’s life is exactly the signal platforms use to judge “this is engaging — show it to more people.” The CTA is doing algorithmic work, not just community-building.

- **The selection.** Once a week, one dream is chosen and gets its own featured video — gently reflected on by Maeve (short) or Nan (longer). The chosen commenter is named/thanked (with permission), which makes everyone else submit hoping to be next.

- **The return hook.** “Come back Sunday to see whose dream I chose” gives people a scheduled reason to return — the habit the whole brand is built on.

- **The content flywheel.** Every submission is a future Short idea, pre-validated by a real person caring enough to write it. You will never run out of topics.

**Safeguard (from the Bible):** Submissions mentioning serious distress or self-harm are never used for content. They get a warm, standard reply pointing to real support. This rule is absolute and lives in your moderation checklist.

**Cadence:** Announce “Dream of the Week” the same day each week (Sunday works — reflective, end-of-week). Pin the winning comment. Keep a running board of every submitted dream as your idea backlog.

**5. The 90-Day Algorithm Plan**

**Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4: Teach the machine (consistency over reach)**

Goal: publish the hero format so consistently that the algorithm learns exactly who to show it to. Do NOT chase virality or change the format based on early numbers.

- Post 1 Short per day, 5–6 days a week (the first 20 concepts in Section 8 cover the first month).

- Same format, same CTA, rotate the three characters. One Dream-of-the-Week feature each week.

- Reply to every comment in the first hour of each post — early engagement compounds.

- Watch retention and comment rate only. Ignore subscriber count for now.

**Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8: Double down on winners**

- Identify the 2–3 topics/hooks with the best retention and make more like them — same format, adjacent symbols.

- Introduce the first long-form Fireside Tale (Nan, 5–8 min) once weekly to start building watch-time depth and the “fall asleep to this” audience.

- Begin cross-posting the same Shorts to TikTok and Instagram Reels (see platform notes) — same asset, tuned captions.

**Phase 3 — Weeks 9–12: Widen the world**

- Add the second and third pillars (Symbols & Signs deep-dives, Moments of Calm) now the hero format is established.

- Start building the email/community capture (the on-ramp to the funnel in Document 3) — but still no hard selling.

- Aim to exit 90 days with a proven format, a clear top-performing character, and a warm, commenting audience — the assets the funnel needs.

**6. Platform Notes**

**YouTube Shorts (primary home)**

- Best for search + long-tail: “dream about teeth meaning” gets searched for years. Write titles and spoken hooks with those exact phrases.

- Feeds the long-form Fireside Tales and the eventual funnel; strongest platform for a durable, monetisable asset.

- Set the AI-content disclosure on every upload (per the Bible’s foundation).

**TikTok (discovery accelerator)**

- Fastest cold-start reach and trend discovery. Great for finding which symbols resonate before committing them to YouTube.

- Label AI content per TikTok’s rules; use its creator AI-label tool.

- Slightly punchier, faster hooks land better here; the twins (Maeve/Jesse) tend to outperform on TikTok.

**Instagram Reels (secondary)**

- Cheap to cross-post the same asset. Strong for saves and shares of the “Moment of Calm” pillar. Lowest priority of the three early on.

**One asset, three homes:** Make each Short once, export clean (no platform watermark), and post natively to all three. Never post a TikTok-watermarked clip to YouTube/Reels — it gets suppressed.

**7. The Metrics That Actually Matter**

- **Average % viewed (retention):** the master metric. Aim to keep climbing; a Short that holds ~70%+ is working. Fix the first 3 seconds first if it’s low.

- **Comment rate:** comments ÷ views. This is your Dream-of-the-Week loop working. Uniquely high for this brand — protect it.

- **Re-watches / loops:** a loopable end frame quietly doubles watch time. Design for it.

- **Shares & saves:** the strongest “this is valuable” signal, especially for Moments of Calm.

- **Follower conversion:** watch it, but later. Subscribers are a lagging result of the metrics above, not a target to chase directly.

**Weekly review ritual:** Every week, list your 3 best and 3 worst Shorts by retention. Ask one question: what did the winners’ first 3 seconds do that the losers didn’t? Make next week’s hooks look like the winners. That single habit is 80% of channel growth.

**9. Batch Production Workflow**

Twenty Shorts sounds like a lot until you batch it. Do it in stages, not one video end-to-end at a time — batching is what makes a one-person (soon two-person) operation produce daily.

- **Script day:** write all 20 scripts to the beat sheet in one sitting (your counselling ear makes the “Turn” genuinely good — that’s your edge).

- **Voice day:** generate all voiceovers in the three locked ElevenLabs voices in one pass.

- **Visual day:** batch-render in Higgsfield (twins) and HeyGen (Nan) using the locked character references from the Bible. Keep prompts/settings identical for consistency.

- **Assembly day:** edit, burn in captions, add the shared intro/outro and CTA end-card, export clean masters.

- **Schedule:** load them into a scheduler so a month posts itself while you build Document 3 (the funnel).
