# Nan Sybil — Voice Design Brief

> How to design her custom ElevenLabs voice. Agreed approach: a **designed
> voice** with weathered, raspy warmth — and let the breath-score
> (`nan-sybil-presence-and-stillness.md`) do the heavy lifting to make it
> uniquely hers. Timbre from the voice; stillness from the writing.

---

## The target (paste into ElevenLabs Voice Design)

An older woman, late 60s–70s. Low-to-mid pitch. A **gentle rasp and grain of
age** — weathered, lived-in, never frail. Warm, unhurried, intimate — she
speaks close to you, never projects. A soft, non-specific old-country /
Northern English lilt. A faint smile in the voice. Kind, wise, calm; the
loveliest grandmother you can imagine, sitting by a fire.

Keywords: *weathered · raspy-warm · low · slow · soft-edged · intimate ·
grandmotherly · a smile in it · unhurried · kind.*

---

## Build path

1. Use **ElevenLabs Voice Design** (text-to-voice from a description) to
   generate several candidates from the target above.
2. Generate 4–6 options; audition each on a real Nan line (below).
3. Pick the one with the warmest natural grain — rasp present but never harsh
   or wobbly.
4. Save it as the **locked** Nan voice. Never swap it once chosen; consistency
   is the whole game.

*(Alternative, if you'd rather use a human timbre: cast a warm older voice
actor who creates and verifies their OWN Professional Voice Clone on their
account and shares it with you under a written agreement. Do not attempt to
clone a real person's voice yourself — ElevenLabs only clones your own verified
voice.)*

---

## Baseline settings

- **Stability:** ~65–72% for her cosy register; nudge to ~70–75% for the deep
  still passages so long pauses don't wobble. Not higher — she'll go flat.
- **Style / exaggeration:** low, ~10–20%. Warmth is restraint.
- **Similarity:** high, once the voice is chosen.
- **Pace:** slow for gravity points, natural for cosy passages. Vary it.
- **Breaks:** punctuation first (the breath-score); `<break>` tags only for the
  big silences, sparingly.

---

## The stillness is NOT in the voice — it's in the writing

The rasp and warmth come from the designed voice. The **weight, the pauses,
the breathing** come from how the script is scored — ellipses, short landing
lines, silence *before* the key word, one-word sentences. See
`nan-sybil-presence-and-stillness.md`. A perfect voice read flat sounds dead;
an ordinary voice scored for the breath sounds like her. Score first.

---

## Test protocol (do this before locking the voice)

1. Render the Nan lines from **Worked Example 02** ("The Thing in the Trees") —
   the fear one — because it has the widest range: cosy open, heavy gravity
   point, tender close.
2. Check the gravity point lands: does the silence *before* "nothing came out"
   carry weight?
3. Apply the **three-take rule** — generate each key line 2–3× and keep the
   warmest.
4. A/B two candidate voices on the same script and pick the one that makes you
   feel the way your nan made you feel. That feeling is the acceptance test.
